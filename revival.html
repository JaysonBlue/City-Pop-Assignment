The Revival of City Pop

After fading from the center of Japanese popular music during the 1990s, City Pop spent years outside of the mainstream. Decades later, the music began finding a completely new audience through the internet. One of the most important parts of this revival was the unexpected worldwide popularity of Mariya Takeuchi's "Plastic Love."

The Plastic Love Phenomenon

From 1984 to the Internet

"Plastic Love" was originally released by Mariya Takeuchi in 1984 as part of her album Variety. At the time, it was simply another recording from the era when City Pop was part of contemporary Japanese popular music.

Decades later, the song began circulating widely online. YouTube uploads and recommendations introduced "Plastic Love" to listeners who had never encountered Japanese City Pop before. Its catchy melody, polished production, and distinctly 1980s atmosphere made it particularly easy for a new generation of listeners to connect with.

The importance of "Plastic Love" was not simply that one song became popular again. It became a doorway. People who discovered the song could then search for Mariya Takeuchi, explore other City Pop artists, and eventually discover an entire catalog of music from the 1970s and 1980s.

The City Pop Rabbit Hole

One of the most interesting parts of the revival was the way the internet allowed listeners to keep discovering music. Finding one song could quickly lead to another through recommendations, playlists, music videos, and online communities.

A listener might begin with "Plastic Love" and then discover another Mariya Takeuchi song. From there, they might encounter Tatsuro Yamashita, Anri, Junko Ohashi, Toshiki Kadomatsu, Masayoshi Takanaka, or artists they had never heard of before. Each discovery created another path into the genre.

This helped change the way City Pop was experienced. Instead of being tied to the Japanese music market of the 1980s, recordings could now be discovered by listeners anywhere in the world, often decades after they were originally released.

How the Internet Helped

YouTube

YouTube became an important way for listeners to encounter older Japanese recordings. Recommendations could lead listeners from one song into an increasingly large collection of related music.

Vaporwave & Future Funk

Internet music communities also experimented with older Japanese recordings through sampling, remixing, and recontextualizing music from the 1980s. These scenes helped bring attention to sounds that had largely been forgotten by mainstream audiences.

Streaming

Streaming services made large catalogs of Japanese music easier to explore. Once listeners found one City Pop song, discovering related artists and albums became much easier.

Why Did City Pop Come Back?

The revival of City Pop was not caused by a single song or a single instance. The internet created an environment where old recordings could be rediscovered, shared, recommended, remixed, and explored by people who were not part of the original audience.

City Pop also fit naturally into the visual culture of the internet. Its album artwork, neon cities, beaches, fashion, and polished sound created a strong atmosphere that could be appreciated even by listeners unfamiliar with Japanese music.

"Plastic Love" became one of the most recognizable doors into this genre, but it was only one part of a much larger musical catalog. The modern revival gave a new generation the opportunity to discover just how much music had been waiting behind that door.
